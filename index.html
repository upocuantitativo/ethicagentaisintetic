Research Methodology

Mixed-methods approach: OSINT + NLP + Manual Verification

Research Objective

This study investigates the emergence of synthetic companies — legal entities created or operated primarily by AI agents with minimal or no human involvement. We aim to: (1) develop a taxonomy of AI-enabled organizational forms, (2) build a detection methodology to identify them, (3) construct a validated international sample, and (4) analyze the ethical, governance, and accountability implications for a high-impact academic article.

Context: Sam Altman (OpenAI) predicted "one-person billion-dollar companies" powered by AI agents. Dario Amodei (Anthropic) expects this by 2026. Meanwhile, cases like HurumoAI (a Wired experiment with fully AI employees), Dictador's robot CEO "Mika", and NetDragon's AI CEO "Tang Yu" show this is already happening. We study the full spectrum: from legitimate agentic organizations to fully synthetic (potentially fraudulent) entities.

Synthetic Score — Detection Algorithm

Each entity receives a composite score (0–100) based on 8 weighted signals. Higher scores indicate greater likelihood of being synthetic:

Score Interpretation

  • 0–30: Likely a real company with legitimate operations. AI is a tool, not the operator.
  • 31–50: Agentic organization — heavy AI use but clear human presence. Needs review.
  • 51–70: Ambiguous — could be heavily automated or partially synthetic. Requires deep verification (Phase 4).
  • 71–85: High probability of synthetic entity. Multiple red flags detected. Priority for human verification.
  • 86–100: Almost certainly synthetic or a shell. AI-generated content, no employees, phantom address, no financial records.

8 Detection Signals

  • S1 (20%): No verified employees (LinkedIn, Glassdoor cross-check)
  • S2 (15%): AI-generated web content (GLTR/GPTZero score)
  • S3 (15%): Virtual/fake registered address
  • S4 (15%): Missing financial records or zero filings
  • S5 (10%): Domain age vs. incorporation date mismatch
  • S6 (10%): Web traffic anomalies (bot ratio, fake sources)
  • S7 (10%): No real product or service delivered
  • S8 (5%): AI references in corporate identity ("AI CEO")

Data Reliability Index (DRI) — How We Measure Confidence

The DRI is a meta-indicator that measures how much we can trust the data collected. It is calculated from 4 components:

Source Diversity (25%)

  • How many independent sources confirm a data point?
  • Single source = low; 3+ sources = high
  • Penalizes over-reliance on one platform

Cross-Validation Rate (25%)

  • % of entities validated by 2+ methods
  • Registry + OSINT + contact = triple validation
  • Target: >60% of sample

Verification Depth (25%)

  • How many of the 7 verification steps completed per entity?
  • 1-2 steps = shallow; 5+ = deep
  • Deeper = more reliable classification

Sample Coverage (25%)

  • Geographic + sector representativeness
  • Min. 20 countries, 5 entity types
  • Avoids US/UK bias in results

Decision Rule: An entity is classified as "Confirmed Synthetic" only if it fails 3 or more verification steps (B–E). If it fails 1–2 steps, it is classified as "Indeterminate" and flagged for additional investigation.           Sample

          • n = 45 observed entities: 19 verified AI-delegated + 26 controls. 44 demonstration rows excluded.
          • Recruitment: 16 via the score pipeline · 29 via manual search (score-independent).
          • Jurisdictions: 19 OECD-traditional · 22 permissive/decentralized · 4 other.
          • 17.8% report zero continuous employees (was reported as 38% on the unaudited sample).

          Inter-rater reliability

          • κ = 0.772, bootstrap 95% CI [0.543, 0.944] over 5,000 resamples. The lower bound falls below 0.70, so the threshold is not established.
          • The κ → 1.00 claim is withdrawn as tautological: removing every disagreement leaves only agreements.
          • All four disagreements involve the Multi-Agent System label — the architecture axis colliding with delegated function and governance. The first-round claim that three fell on the Synthetic/Shell frontier was wrong.
          • Only 10 double-coded cases survive into the analytic sample; reliability is a limitation, not a strength.

          Leakage removed

          • The synthetic score is excluded from every model. Reported only as a detector.
          • Prior specification (score included): RF 0.933. Primary (score excluded): RF 0.858. The gap is what the earlier figure owed to leakage.
          • Adding the AI-text detector score lowers accuracy, consistent with it being noise.
          • 15 manually-added rows carry the data-entry default score of exactly 50 — flagged in the released dataset.

          Score as a detector

          • All observed entities: AUC = 0.921 [0.830, 0.985].
          • On the 29 entities it played no part in selecting: AUC = 0.900 [0.783, 1.000].
          • Caveat: those scores were analyst-assigned under the same rubric, so this establishes independence of selection, not of analyst judgement.

          Holdouts

          • temporal youngest half: 0.696 vs 0.565 majority.
          • jurisdictional permissive decentralized: 0.682 vs 0.545 majority.
          • source family press: 0.818 vs 0.682 majority.
          • source family chain analytics: 0.824 vs 0.588 majority.
          • channel stage4 manual as independent test: 0.828 vs 0.690 majority.
          • Group-aware CV by source family: 0.848 (SD 0.092).
          • Transfer to unseen periods and jurisdictions settles near 0.68–0.70 — the honest expectation for a new cohort.

          Withdrawn from the first round

          • Leave-one-class-out — removing a class makes the problem easier; it tests nothing.
          • Threshold sweep — close to tautological, since the score was built to select those cases.
          • Solvency gate — a workflow discipline is not statistical identification, and no timestamped pre-specification exists.
          • Four-class classifier and the 1/6 baseline — withdrawn with the nominal class structure.

          Headline takeaways

          • Detectable, more modestly. AI-delegated entities separate from human-led controls at 0.858 against a 0.578 majority baseline, on features that played no part in selecting them.
          • Leakage quantified. Removing the selection score costs 0.075 accuracy. The earlier 0.92–0.94 headline is superseded.
          • Multidimensional, not four classes. Five separately coded dimensions; 15 of 216 profiles occupied; the coders' own disagreements show why a single axis failed.
          • Two propositions fail. Clustering does not recover the coding against a pre-set band, and reliability does not establish κ ≥ 0.70. Reported as failures.
          • Auditable. Every row, including the 44 excluded, ships with its provenance, registry verdict and search log.
